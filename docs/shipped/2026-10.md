### Estimated power for outdoor rides

2026-10-26: outdoor rides without a power meter get average and normalized power estimated from speed, elevation and body weight, so they count on the same TSS scale as trainer rides.

- `services/power_estimate.py`: rolling resistance + gravity + air drag + kinetic-energy change ÷ drivetrain efficiency, floored at 0 when coasting or braking. Hybrid profile (the Canyon Pathlite): CdA 0.40 m², Crr 0.006, bike 13 kg, 97% drivetrain, air 1.2 kg/m³. Rider weight is the latest logged weight on or before the ride (80 kg default). Speed is smoothed over 9 samples (watch-recorded speed jumps ~0.4 m/s each second and the acceleration term turned that into phantom watts); grade comes from altitude over ±10 samples, capped at ±25%.
- Only `Ride`s with a recorded `velocity_smooth` stream, no `trainer` flag and no confirmed power meter. Old exports whose speed would have to come from GPS distance disagreed with Strava's estimate by ~30% and keep the HR fallback. Estimates are cached per process by detail fetch stamp and weight; nothing is written to the database.
- Load: `build_training_load_summary` scores those rides as `estimated_power_tss` (NP vs the FTP in force that day, same formula as a measured ride); without an FTP they keep HR TRIMP. Model is now `hybrid_hrtss_tss_v3` and reports `estimated_power` (model, ride count, error band). The load forecast picks this up through the summary. On a copy of the live data: 59 rides in the last 120 days use it; fitness today 50.5 → 48.5, summer ~3–5 lower (heat-inflated HR had overstated easy commutes).
- Ride type: `ride_balance` reads the estimated stream on the same power thresholds before falling back to HR; basis `estimated_power`, and the reason says "(estimated power)" / "~W".
- Ride detail: `estimated_power` (avg, NP, IF, TSS, weight, ±15%) next to Strava's own estimate; the page shows "Est. power ~X W · NP ~Y W" and "Est. TSS", both flagged as estimated, replacing the Strava chip when present.
- Never in records, power curve or FTP: the estimate is never stored as a `watts` stream, so `personal_records`, `power_trends` and the FTP estimate (all gated by `_confirmed_power_meter`) cannot see it. A test proves a hard outdoor ride estimated above FTP leaves power records and the FTP estimate empty.
- Calibration (`calibration_report`): no outdoor ride has a real meter, so it runs on VirtualRides, which have both measured power and the speed and elevation it produced ("virtual" profile: CdA 0.26, Crr 0.004, no drivetrain loss). Group rides, pacer rides and meetups are left out because Zwift drafting makes them read 15–35% high. Stated band: median ride NP within ±15%. On the live copy: 49 rides, median NP error 9.3% (bias +7.6%), 80% within 17.8%, average power median error 10.8%. Against Strava's own estimated average on outdoor rides the hybrid model reads ~17–22% lower; Strava's number is an estimate too, so it is a cross-check, not truth.
- Not done: wind, drafting and surface are unknown; no bike-type setting (profiles exist in code, hybrid is used); calibration is not shown in the UI.
- Verified: `test_power_estimate` (17 tests: physics terms, coasting, acceleration, NP, eligibility, streams untouched, load source, HR fallback without FTP, records/FTP exclusion, ride type, calibration in and out of band), a ride-detail test, a frontend chip test, `just check` and the frontend build.

### Heart-rate load on the TSS scale

2026-10-26: fitness, fatigue and form no longer mix raw heart-rate TRIMP with power TSS, so HR-only rides stop counting double.

- Fitness/fatigue/form mixed two load scales: power TSS (1 h at FTP = 100) and raw Banister TRIMP, which ran about 2× higher for the same ride (median 2.1 over 71 rides with both). HR-only outdoor rides inflated Aug–Sep fitness to ~110, and switching to trainer rides with power made it look like a steep fitness slide.
- `hr_load_scales` now puts TRIMP on the TSS scale. Rides use the median power-TSS/TRIMP ratio of the last year's rides that have both (at least 5, clamped 0.25–1.0; 0.48 today). Runs, and rides without enough pairs, anchor an hour at threshold heart rate to 100 (0.67 for runs today). Both apply to stream TRIMP and the avg-HR fallback.
- Hikes and walks with a heart rate are scored the same way on the run scale instead of a flat 0.6 / 0.35 per minute (an 8.5 h hike at 111 bpm went from 308 to 170). Without HR they keep the per-minute rate, which the load forecast still uses for planned trip days.
- The model is now `hybrid_hrtss_tss_v2` and reports `hr_load_scale`. Relative effort, session read and ride detail keep raw TRIMP (they compare like with like).
- Live chart: fitness ~60 through Aug–Sep, ~50 now, form +7 today (was 80 / 80 / 0). Verified with 3 new tests in `test_training_load_history` and `just check`.

### Injury-aware weekly planning

2026-10-26: the plan agent now plans around injuries logged on the Recovery page, after it put the lower-body Workout D on a sore ITBS knee two days after it was logged.

- Prompt (`scripts/coach_helper.py` `INJURY_GUIDANCE`, used by both "Plan this week" and plan feedback revisions): an active `recovery_issues` entry outranks the strength rotation, leg volume, mountain re-entry and the winter arc. No lift loads a protected area; for a knee, hip or heel the lower-body workout is skipped this week (it stays next in the rotation) and replaced with an upper-body workout, and extra upper-body sets or a third upper-body session are welcome. Rides, runs and hikes that load the area stay easy with a written pain check (0-2/10 at rest, else walk or rehab) and no quality work while pain is 3+. With a rehab routine, 2-3 `Recovery` sessions titled "Rehab: <area>" list every exercise and dose in details. The mountain re-entry rule no longer asks for a lower-body lift while a leg area is injured.
- Context: `recovery.coaching_summary` adds `protected_areas`, the routine's `do` list and `rehab_exercises` (name and dose) to each active issue in `get_recent_context`.
- Lift top-up: `recovery.issue_body_areas` turns active issues into protected areas, merged with the Athlete page entries. A protected knee, hip or heel now adds no leg sets at all (calf raises and Romanian deadlifts were still being added); the legs row reads "resting" on the volume panel.
- Not done: rehab sessions are plain text in the day's details, not a guided routine with tick-offs.
- Verified with unit tests in `backend/tests/test_strength_volume.py`, `backend/tests/test_recovery.py` and `scripts/test_coach_helper.py`, plus `just check`.

### Strava photos on the trip recap

2026-10-25: photos uploaded to Strava for mountain hikes show on the trip recap (`/mountains/trips/<first day>`). `services/activity_photos.py` reads Strava's activity photos endpoint, which gives each photo's GPS location (from the phone), the time it was taken, a caption and size-specific CDN URLs. The CDN links may not last, so each photo is downloaded once in two sizes (256 px for pins and the filmstrip, 1600 px for the lightbox) into `photos/` next to the database. They are kept out of the database so the backups stay small (about 450 KB per photo). Metadata lives in `activity_photos`, and the photo count last seen per activity in `activity_photo_syncs`. Image requests to the CDN go without the Strava token.

- When: the regular Strava import fetches photos for listed foot activities in a mapped range whose `total_photo_count` differs from the stored count. The Mountains **Sync hikes** pass (`sync_tracks`) runs last: it fetches every stored mountain track never checked, newest first, and rechecks tracks from the last 10 days, because photos are often added a day or two later. It stops at Strava's rate limit and reports `photos_added` / `photos_pending`. Photos deleted on Strava are removed, and placeholder entries for uploads still processing are skipped until they're ready.
- API: each recap activity carries `photos` (id, location, times, caption, size); the trip has a `photos` count. `GET /trails/photos/{id}` (`?size=thumb`) serves the file with a long private cache. Ids are checked against the UUID pattern before they become file names.
- Map: a round thumbnail pin per photo with the day's colour as its border. Photos whose pins would overlap at the current zoom share one pin (the earliest photo, with a count badge), regrouped on every zoom. Hovering a pin highlights its day's route and its photos in the filmstrip; clicking opens the lightbox there. A legend button hides and shows the pins.
- Filmstrip ("Along the way"): every photo of the trip in the order taken, grouped by day, with the day and clock time. Hovering highlights the pin and the day; photos without GPS appear only here. A "photos taken" count joins the hero numbers.
- Lightbox (`components/trails/PhotoLightbox.vue`): the full-size photo with day, date, time, outing name and caption; ←/→ and buttons step through the whole trip, Esc or the backdrop closes it, and neighbouring photos are preloaded.
- Verified with 5 backend tests (both sizes stored without the token, re-sync keeps files and drops deleted photos, listing-count trigger, new-and-recent track pass with the rate limit, recap and image API) and 2 frontend helper tests. The existing sync tests' mocks now answer the photos endpoint. Checked in the browser at 1512 px against a scratch copy of the database with the 20–24 Oct Karkonosze trip's 34 real photos synced (all with GPS): 15 pins at trip zoom, splitting further on zoom-in, the filmstrip, pin/filmstrip hover, the lightbox with keyboard stepping, and the photo toggle. The live database has no photos yet; the first **Sync hikes** fetches them for the 99 stored mountain tracks (about 2 requests each, so it may take a couple of syncs because of the rate limit).

### Gentle strength phases

- Kinds: `volume` is a label only; `hold` pauses load jumps; `deload` (a week, two at most) trims working sets to about 60% (`deload_sets`: 3 → 2, 4 → 2, 5 → 3, never under 1) and pauses load jumps. Phases cannot overlap.
- They only nudge existing rules. Load holds are rule 6 in `progression_guard.guard_steps` (held reason `phase`, applied after the effort, big-jump and mountain re-entry rules). Set trimming happens only when a session starts (`strength_workouts.start_session` via `session_set_count`). Saved workouts, exercises and stall-block rewrites are untouched, and plans are not changed, so the weekly lift count toward the 3x anchor stays the same.
- Suggestions: each winter-arc lighter week from this week on that no phase covers is offered as a deload week (`suggestions`, computed and never stored). You confirm it on the card (saved with `source` `arc_lighter_week`).
- API: `GET/POST /strength/workouts/phases`, `DELETE /strength/workouts/phases/{id}`. The dashboard payload and coach context carry `strength_phase` (the current phase with a headline).
- UI: a compact "Strength phase" card at the top of the Workout studio side panel (current phase or "Off", planned phases with Remove, suggested deload weeks with Confirm, an Add form). The Dashboard's Load & recovery card shows the current phase's headline.
- Not done: a deload week's sessions count toward stall detection like any other session, so a long hold could bring a plateau suggestion earlier. The lift/hard-ride clash rule is unchanged.
- Verified with `test_strength_phases.py` (8 tests: no phase, deload sets and holds, hold, volume, out-of-range phase, set rounding, arc suggestions until confirmed, overlap and length checks). ### Food targets follow the planned week

2026-10-15: implements the `food-targets-follow-the-block` idea. `services/food_targets.py` reads the saved plan for the Monday–Sunday week around the selected Food day; `GET /nutrition/food` (`food_log.build_food_day`) now carries `planned_week`, and `target` gains `training_planned`, `day_kind` and `carbs_g`.

- Day kinds: key ride (hard ride by the lift/ride clash rule), long ride (intent `long` or 120+ min), lift (not mobility), easy ride (also recovery rides, runs and other work) and rest; a day takes its biggest session.
- Energy: baseline (resting × daily life, plus the gain surplus) + training. Finished days use logged training only, as before. Today and later days use the larger of logged and planned training; planned = net METs by kind (key 9.5, long 6.5, easy 6, recovery 4, lift 3.5, run 8.8) × weight × planned minutes. - Eat-more hint: planned ride hours (saved plan, else the winter-arc target) at least 1 h and 15% above the last 4 full weeks' average ride hours. Shown as "Eat more this week" on the new **This week's targets** card (Food side column: kind, kcal and carbs per day), and in the coach's `get_nutrition` context as `planned_week`.
- The Carbs tile shows the day's carb target ("Key ride target"); the burn bar's training tooltip says when training is a planned estimate.
- Verified with `test_food_targets.py` (7 tests: kinds and carbs differ for rest/lift/easy/key/long, today counts the planned session, finished days ignore the plan, logged beats a smaller plan, big-week hint and carb step, steady week without hint, lighter week never below baseline); backend 924 and frontend 168 tests plus the build pass. ### Block recap at the end of every arc block

2026-10-15: implements the `block-end-review` idea. Every finished winter-arc block has a celebratory recap at `/plan/blocks/<start date>`, built in `services/block_recap.py` (`GET /plans/winter-arc/blocks/{start}/recap`). It opens the day after the block's last Sunday (before that: 409 "still running; opens on …").

- Hero: a headline that says what went right or short ("Build had the hours, not the key rides."), then count-up numbers: hours ridden of target, key rides done of planned, longest run of on-target weeks, days on the bike.
- Visuals, no tables: a week-by-week column chart (dashed outline = target hours, fill = ridden, green = on target, "lost" flag from `arc_adjust.week_disruption`) with key-ride dots per week; key rides per type as filled/hollow dots; best 1/5/20-minute power in the block against the 6 weeks before (from the power-trends efforts); and a drift sparkline over the block's steady rides with the 5% line (`build_aerobic_decoupling` on the block's window, indoor or outdoor, whichever had more).
- Planned vs done: planned = what the block asked each week (`week_target`, lighter weeks scaled); done key rides = rides classified from what was ridden (`ride_balance.classify_ride`: power, then heart rate, then intent), at most what each week asked. A week is on target at 80%+ of its hours with every key ride.
- Suggestions for the next block (at most two, never an increase; lost weeks are left out): hours under 80% and the next block asks for more → hold it at this block's hours; under 65% → trim it to the usual ridden week (rounded to 0.5 h); under 60% of key rides and the next block asks for 2+ quality rides a week → one fewer of its most common quality ride. Nothing changes until "Apply to …" is pressed (`POST …/recap/decision`, which must still match the recomputed suggestion, else 409); "Keep as planned" dismisses. Decisions are stored in `block_recap_decisions` and shown as "Applied / Kept as planned on …"; clearing the arc clears them. The last block of the arc gets no suggestions.
- Not enough data: fewer than 3 rides of 20+ min in the block shows "Not enough data" with the count, still draws the honest zeros, and skips power, drift and suggestions. Power and drift each have their own "Not enough data" line when the block lacks power-meter or steady rides.
- Plan: the Winter arc card links every finished block's recap ("Block recaps: Base · Build") and calls out the latest one for 21 days ("Build is done. See how the block went →").
- Verified with `test_block_recap.py` (hours hold applied only on confirm, stale value 409, no second apply; missed key rides then dismiss leaves the arc; thin block; running and unknown blocks; routes; a decision survives an arc re-save). Backend 917 and frontend 168 tests plus the build pass. On a scratch copy of the database with a past three-block test arc over real Aug–Sep rides, both recaps rendered at 1512 px (Build: 30.8 of 24 h, 1 of 4 sweet-spot rides, +44 W at 20 min, drift 7.8% median), Apply changed only the copy's next block, and an empty 2012 block showed the not-enough-data state. ### Winter arc adapts to lost weeks

2026-10-15: implements the `arc-adapts-to-interruptions` idea. When a block loses a week or more to illness, a mountain trip or busy days, the Winter arc card on Plan suggests extending the block or accepting the loss. Built in `services/arc_adjust.py`.

- Lost week: each finished week of the block that holds last week is checked for disrupted days: sick days, illness return windows (`illness_return.past_return_dates`), Mountains days, mountain re-entry windows (`mountain_reentry.past_reentry_dates`) and any other life-load tag. A week is lost with 4+ disrupted days, or with 2+ when less than half its ride hours were ridden. Two busy days with riding on track do not count.
- Suggestion card (amber, above the strip): which weeks were lost and why ("2 sick days, 5 illness return days"), and a before/after table of every block's dates, length and lighter weeks with the changed cells highlighted. "Extend by N weeks" (N = lost weeks, at most 2) lengthens the block and moves every later block back by the same amount; lighter weeks stay at the same week number inside each block, and gaps between blocks are kept. "Accept and move on" keeps the dates.
- Nothing changes until a button is pressed. `GET/PUT/DELETE /plans/winter-arc` now also return `adjustment` (`suggestion`, `last`); `POST /plans/winter-arc/adjustment` confirms (it must still match the current suggestion, else 409) and `POST /plans/winter-arc/adjustment/undo` undoes. Each decision is stored in `winter_arc_adjustments` with the arc before and after. The last one stays on the card for 14 days with Undo; an extension can only be undone while the arc is still as it left it. Undo brings the suggestion back. Decided weeks are not asked about again; a newly lost week brings a new card. Clearing the arc clears the decisions.
- No cramming: a missed key ride from a lost week no longer carries into the next week (`carry_blocked` "that week was lost", shown in the week note). Saved weekly plans are not refitted when an extension moves blocks; their arc pill follows the new dates.
- Verified with `test_arc_adjust.py` (illness, mountain trip with re-entry, busy fortnight extending by 2, light disruption not lost, accept then a newly lost week, lost week not carried, stale confirm 409, undo after a hand edit refused, routes). Backend 912 and frontend 168 tests plus the build pass. On a scratch copy of the database with a three-block test arc, the real 1–6 Oct sick period produced "Base lost 2 weeks (28 Sep, 5 Oct) to illness"; Extend moved the blocks (season timeline and week pill followed) and Undo restored them, at 1512 px and 375 px. ### Detailed streams for the whole Strava history

2026-10-13: after importing Strava history back to 2013, 2,109 older runs and rides had only summary numbers, so their fastest-distance records, season bests, charts and maps were missing. The existing "Fetch next 12" backfill skips runs without heart rate, which is most of them before 2022.

- `services/strava_history_backfill.py` fetches full streams in the background: runs first, newest first, in batches of up to 60 about every 16 minutes. It stops a batch while 35% of Strava's 15-minute or daily limit is still free, so syncing and activity pages keep working; at the default 1,000 requests a day that is about 3 days for the whole history.
- Each activity is tried once and recorded in `strava_history_backfill` (fetched, no_streams for manual entries, or failed with up to 3 attempts). A rate-limit error or a network problem pauses the batch without marking anything. Progress is committed after every activity.
- Streams are cached like detail-page fetches (`activity_details.streams_json`). A training-load stream summary is stored only when the streams carry heart rate or power; without either it would count the whole session as easy aerobic time.
- Switched on and off from Data & Sync ("Detailed history" card with progress and what happens next: "Fetching a batch of up to 60 now", "Next batch of up to 60 about 15:41", or the pause reason; between batches the count holds still on purpose), or `POST /integrations/strava/history-backfill {"enabled": true}`; `GET` gives the status. - Full streams take about 150 KB per run and 300 KB per ride: the database grows from about 226 MB to about 600 MB.

### Aerobic fitness without a test

2026-10-05: `services/aerobic_decoupling.py` tracks heart-rate drift (Pw:HR decoupling) and power per heartbeat (W/bpm) on steady rides, so winter base fitness shows up from ordinary Zone 2 rides with no FTP test. It reads the cached power and heart-rate streams through the `power_trends` segment parser; nothing is estimated or written as FTP.

- Qualifying: Ride/VirtualRide of 45+ min with measured power (same `device_watts` / VirtualRide-backfill rule as the power profile), not planned as tempo, intervals or race-specific. The first 10 min and last 5 min are trimmed; power and heart rate must cover 90% of the rest, zero watts at most 10%, normalized/average power at most 1.10, and average power at most 80% of the latest stored FTP (skipped with no FTP). Every other ride in the window is listed with its reason.
- Per ride: efficiency over the trimmed window, and decoupling = efficiency drop from the first to the second half (under 5% is steady).
- Trend, per environment (indoor = VirtualRide or Strava `trainer`; outdoor otherwise): needs 3 qualifying rides, else `unavailable`. Direction comes from heart rate at the same power, a least-squares fit of `avg_hr ~ avg_watts + day` once there are 5+ rides with a 10 W spread, because raw W/bpm also rises on harder rides; with fewer it falls back to the efficiency slope (±2%). A same-power comparison (latest ride against the earliest within ±7% power) gives the coach line, e.g. "Same power, 7 bpm lower than on 4 Sep".
- API: `GET /metrics/aerobic-decoupling?weeks=8..26` (default 12). Coach: `aerobic_fitness` in `get_recent_context` (trends, last 5 rides, rules), and MCP `get_aerobic_fitness_trend` on both servers.
- UI: "05 / Your aerobic base", the last section on Trends → Cycling power. A two-line heading ("Same power. Lower heart rate."), a hero verdict (bpm change at the same power, with direction pill and three mini stats), a "then and now" pair of rides at similar power, two single-axis charts (power per heartbeat with a dashed trend line; drift with the steady zone under 5% shaded), a ride table with half-by-half heart rate and drift chips, and the excluded rides with reasons. Indoor/outdoor and 8/12/26-week toggles sit top right.
- Real data (12 weeks to 2026-10-05): 7 indoor rides qualify, about 4.6 bpm lower at the same power over 17 days. No outdoor ride qualifies yet because outdoor rides have no power meter.

### Return-to-run tracker

2026-10-05: `services/return_to_run.py` runs a six-stage return to running, driven by symptom scores: run/walk 1:2 → 2:1 → 5:1 → continuous 20 min → continuous 30–40 min → back to normal. The athlete switches it on with a starting stage and the symptom to watch (default heel). It never edits the plan.

- Scores: "during" and "next morning" (0–10) per run in `run_symptom_checks` (`PUT /activities/{id}/symptoms`). The feedback form's pain score stands in for "during" when no symptom score was logged.
- Replay since the start date. Clean (≤ 2 during and the next morning): 2 in a row advance a stage, and the latest needs its morning score. Flare (≥ 4 during or ≥ 3 the next morning): one stage down. A break of 21+ days between runs, or since the last run, also costs a stage. Otherwise the stage holds.
- Next step: needs a score, needs the morning score, flare, rest day (one full day between runs at stages 1–3), ready, or graduated (3 clean full runs in a row at stage 6, meaning symptoms look resolved).
- Suggested start: the last pain-free run's duration maps to a stage, minus one after 3+ weeks off; recent pain above 2/10 starts at stage 1. On real data this suggests stage 3 (28 min at 0/10 on 9 Sep, 26 days ago).

### "What worked" memory

### Pre-session brief

2026-10-04: `services/session_brief.py` gives each planned session a short, coach-style brief with three parts: purpose, how it should feel (an RPE range plus a description) and when to bail. It is deterministic and never changes the plan.

- Rides: templates per workout category (recovery, endurance, tempo, sweet spot, threshold, VO2 max). Watt targets come from the structured workout and the stored FTP; heart-rate caps come from the configured zones.
- Endurance rides anchor the drift rule to the athlete's own data. `usual_hr_at_power` takes the median HR while 30 s power is within ±8% of the target, skipping the first 10 minutes, across the last 90 days (3+ rides needed). The rule is "HR more than 10 bpm above that at the same power". If the usual HR sits above zone 2, a note says to ride by HR.
- If the stored FTP is stale, a note gives the work watts at the recent FTP estimate from the records wall.
- Runs: HR cap, walk-break and pain rules. Strength: reps in reserve, load drop and joint-pain swap. Recovery-type sessions use the title to choose between a recovery ride and mobility.
- Plan guardrail sentences (stop / skip / cut / ease off, up to 180 characters) come first. The rules also cover an active recovery issue (unless the plan already does) and a low morning check-in. At most 3 bail rules are shown.
- Sick days get no brief, since guided home sessions replace the plan.
- `session_briefs` in `GET /dashboard`, plus `GET /session-brief?day=`. The Today card shows the brief in place of the old sentence-split guide, which remains as a fallback.

### Personal best wall

2026-10-02: `services/personal_records.py` builds an all-time records wall from local data. It scans the cached streams of every ride and run, not just activities whose detail page was opened. Per-activity results are cached in `activity_record_efforts` and refreshed when the detail row's `updated_at` changes.

- Bike power: 5 s plus the existing power-profile durations (15 s–60 min), indoor and outdoor combined. 5 s is computed here so the benchmark radar is unchanged.
- Bike distance: 5K, 10K, 20K, 40K, 50K, 100K, longest ride and biggest climb. Outdoor and indoor (VirtualRide or `trainer`) are separate lists because indoor speed is simulated.
- Run: Strava's distances from 400 m to marathon, plus longest run. Distances not yet covered are listed as locked.
- Lifts: estimated 1RM (Epley, working sets of up to 12 reps), heaviest set, and most reps or added weight for bodyweight movements. Exercises need 3+ sessions.
- Streaks: current, longest and milestones at 7–365 days.
- Each record has date, activity, indoor/outdoor, average HR, time of day (where a start time is known), previous best, top 3 (ties go to the earlier date) and a progression derived chronologically.
- FTP estimate: the higher of 95% of the best 20 min and the best 60 min from the last 90 days, shown beside the stored FTP and never written to it.

- Activity Detail best efforts now use the same distances and the same linear fastest-window search (`DETAIL_DERIVED_VERSION` v2). The old per-window scan took about 43 s for 12 activities; the new one takes 0.07 s with matching results.
