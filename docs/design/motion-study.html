Motion Study

Direction C (Coaching), round 5, next to what ships today. The figure now uses real body proportions, and the runner's arms and upper body were rebuilt from run-cycle reference. Use 0.25× or the frame buttons to judge timing, and Arcs to see the paths joints travel.

What changed

  • Round 5: the seat. Every joint used to be a ball centred on its pivot, with the glute as one more ball, so the hips read like an action figure. The seat is now one smooth curve from the lower back, out over the buttock and into the back of the thigh.
  • Round 5: the run. The swing leg follows running joint-angle curves: the knee folds as the thigh comes forward so the heel tucks under the seat, and the thigh peaks at about 34° before pulling back to land. The foot hinges against the shin instead of the ground, the toes bend at the ball at push-off, the body leans 10° forward, and the hands swing low and mostly backward.
  • Round 4: glutes. The hips were a tube centred on the hip joint. Now the glutes sit behind the joint and follow the thigh, so the seat pushes back in a squat and rounds off when standing. They light up as the working muscle in the squat.
  • Round 4: less stiff. Every move now uses a minimum-jerk speed curve (how real lifts and reaches accelerate and slow), not a constant-speed middle. Holds have breathing, a little balance sway and wobble; the spine gives slightly. The runner's pelvis, shoulders and trunk rotate and pitch more, and the spine gives on each landing.
  • Round 3: feet are shoes. The ankle sits over the rear third of the foot, with a rounded heel, a sole tapering to the toe and an instep sloping back to the ankle. Before, the foot was a flat bar hanging under the shin.
  • Round 3: side plank, and less clutter. The new side plank pivots about the feet and the elbow, so nothing slides; the hold sags a touch, then re-braces. The runner's landing-distance marker is gone.
  • Round 2: smaller head, shorter trunk. Sized from standard body proportions (trunk about 0.29 of height, head about 0.13, leg about 0.49). Before, the trunk was 15% too long and the head 20% too big for the legs.
  • Round 2: a shaped trunk. Hips, a narrower waist and a chest, instead of one thick tube. Thighs taper to the knee, shins have a calf, feet have a heel, hands end in a fist.
  • Round 2: runner arms and foot strike. The foot lands about 34 cm ahead on a soft knee and leaves the ground without locking. Longer upper arms on a clean pendulum from the shoulder, opposite the legs: the hand travels from the hip pocket back to chest height in front, and the elbow closes a beat after the arm comes forward.
  • Round 2: runner head. Carried slightly ahead of the trunk and steadier than the hips.

Rehab demos

recovery/exercise-demos.mjs, ported 1:1 in ExerciseDemos.swift

  1. Even timingEvery move uses the same symmetric cosine ease, so lifting, lowering and settling all have the same rhythm. A 2 s lift and a 3 s eccentric lower should feel different.
  2. Straight linesPoses blend hand and foot positions in straight lines and IK fills in the joints. In the straight leg raise that chord is shorter than the leg, so the knee bends about 37° mid-lift and stays 17° bent at the top.
  3. No overlapArms, torso, head and feet start and stop on the same frame. Nothing leads and nothing trails, so the body moves like one rigid puppet.
  4. Dead holdsA hold repeats the same pose, so the figure freezes. A moving hold (breathing, slight strain drift) keeps it alive.
  5. No anticipationNo brace before the move (quad set, toes up, inhale) and no settle after it lands.
  6. TwinningLeft and right limbs move identically, 2 px apart, which reads as mechanical.
  7. Squat pathThe hips slide on one straight diagonal and the head nods with the torso. A real squat sits back first, then down, with a level gaze.

Cadence runner

running/runner-gait.mjs + RunnerFigure.vue

  1. Bolted-on torsoFixed 7° lean. Shoulders and head ride exactly on the hips, with no lag and no counter-rotation.
  2. Rigid pelvisBoth legs hang from one point: no pelvic rotation and no drop on the swing side.
  3. Arms on railsEach arm is a fixed linear copy of the opposite thigh on the same frame, elbow locked near 90°. No lag, no forearm follow-through.
  4. Nodding headThe head bobs 100% with the hips. Runners hold the head steadier than the pelvis.
  5. Flat approachOnly a 6° toe-up blip mid-swing. There's no clear dorsiflexion before the foot lands.
  6. Late-swing skimThe ankle is clamped to ground height in late swing, so the foot can skim the floor and then snap to the touchdown pose.
  7. Slide riskstanceLen is capped at 50 px. Above about 0.86 m of ground covered per contact (e.g. 3.6 m/s with 240 ms contact), the planted foot moves slower than the ground and appears to slide.

The stance and swing leg mechanics are already solid. The fixes are mostly above the hips.
